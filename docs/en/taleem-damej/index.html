Inclusive Education

Inclusive Education in Jordan: From Enrollment to Genuine Participation

Inclusive education is not just placing a student with a disability in the classroom. It is designing a learning environment that removes barriers, provides appropriate accommodations, and measures the student's participation and progress alongside their peers. This includes reviewing teaching and assessment methods, admissions policies, and extracurricular activities, so that genuine participation becomes the standard for success, not just school enrollment. This support also includes remote consulting for schools and families across the GCC.

What Does Effective Inclusion Mean?

Inclusion starts from the student's right to access and participate in education, then turns into daily procedures within the classroom and school. Success is not measured by attendance alone, but by the student's ability to communicate, learn, build relationships, and participate in activities.

  • Identifying educational and environmental barriers
  • Classroom accommodations linked to the student's need
  • Clear roles for the family, teacher, and support team
  • Measurable progress tracking

Assessing a School's Readiness for Inclusion

A readiness assessment reviews school policy, physical and informational access, staff experience, the referral and support pathway, how individual plans are prepared, and communication with families. The result is not a generic report, but an actionable list of priorities.

  • Reviewing policies and procedures
  • Analyzing classroom practices
  • Identifying training gaps
  • A gradual improvement plan

How Can a Family Support the Decision to Include?

A family helps when it shares realistic information about strengths and needs, and agrees with the school on specific goals and stable communication channels. It also needs clear indicators to know whether the learning environment is genuinely supporting their child.

Criteria for Assessing a School's Readiness for Inclusion

Before discussing a development plan, a school needs a realistic picture of where it currently stands.

  • Clarity of the mechanism for identifying support for each student in the general classroom
  • Existing prior teacher training on classroom accommodations
  • A coordinator or follow-up team for individual cases
  • Accessibility of the building, facilities, and extracurricular activities
  • A regular communication channel with families of students with disabilities

Frequently Asked Questions

Short, clear answers

Is every school that admits a student with a disability an inclusive school?

No. Admission is only a first step. An inclusive school provides access, accommodations, and support, and tracks the student's participation and progress within an environment that respects diversity.

What's the difference between inclusion and special education?

Special education includes specialized services and strategies, while inclusive education organizes the delivery of support within a shared learning environment as much as possible, while removing barriers to participation.

Can the entire school staff be trained?

Yes, and training can be designed for administration, teachers, and the support team based on the school's readiness level and the situations it faces.
