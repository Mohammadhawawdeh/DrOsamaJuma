Inclusive Education

Inclusive Education in Jordan: A Practical Guide for Families and Schools

Inclusive education doesn't just mean admitting a student to the school - it means providing genuine opportunities for participation, learning, and communication within the classroom. This guide offers clear steps to help families and schools move from general intentions to measurable practices.

By Dr. Osama Juma — PhD in Special Education, The University of Jordan · Published: August 31, 2026 · 5 min read

What Does Inclusive Education Mean?

It means organizing the learning environment so students learn together, while providing the accommodations and support each student needs. Inclusion doesn't assume the child alone must adapt to a fixed system - it calls on the school to review its teaching methods, activities, assessment, and communication.

The goal isn't to erase individual differences, but to engage with them respectfully and turn them into information that helps the teacher plan.

What Are the Signs of a Genuinely Inclusive School?

Ask how the school identifies a student's needs, how tasks are adapted, who follows up on progress, and how the family participates in decisions. A ready school can give practical examples, not just general statements about welcoming everyone.

Positive signs include: clear goals, collaboration between teachers, varied alternatives for participation, an accessible environment, and a regular mechanism for reviewing results.

What's a Practical Plan to Get Started?

Start by describing the student's strengths and educational needs, then choose two or three goals that can be observed and measured. Agree on responsibilities, the timeframe, and how data will be collected, and set a near-term review date.

If no benefit appears, adjust the strategy rather than blaming the student. Inclusion is a continuous improvement process, not a one-time decision.

Note: This content is general awareness/educational material. Determining the most appropriate support requires understanding the student's needs and learning environment, and collaboration with the relevant team.
