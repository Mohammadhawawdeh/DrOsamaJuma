Classroom Support

Supporting Students With Learning Disabilities in the Inclusive Classroom

A learning disability does not mean a student has lower intellectual ability - it means a different way of processing information that needs different classroom strategies, not just more effort.

By Dr. Osama Juma — PhD in Special Education, The University of Jordan · Published: September 22, 2026 · 5 min read

What's the Difference Between “Can't” and “Needs a Different Approach”?

Many people confuse a learning disability with laziness or low intelligence. A student with dyslexia, for example, may easily understand an idea verbally but face real difficulty decoding the written word - a fundamental difference that changes what support is appropriate.

Start by identifying exactly where the difficulty appears: reading, writing, math, or organization and working memory - each type needs a different strategy.

What Are the Practical Classroom Accommodations?

Give extra time for reading and writing, and present instructions in writing and verbally together. Breaking a long task into numbered steps reduces the load on working memory and improves actual completion.

Use simple tools like audio recording of the lesson, or text-to-speech software, without treating them as “unfair help” - they are a means of accessing content, not a shortcut around effort.

How Do You Assess the Student Without Lowering Expectations?

Separate measuring the required skill - such as understanding a science topic - from measuring the reading skill itself, if reading isn't the focus of the assessment. An oral test or extra time may give a more accurate picture of the student's actual understanding.

Adjusting how content is accessed doesn't mean simplifying the content itself; the goal is for the student to show what they actually know, without a reading or writing difficulty masking their real ability.

Note: A learning disability is a diagnosis determined by a qualified specialist through appropriate assessment; this content is general educational material for classroom practice and does not replace specialized assessment.
