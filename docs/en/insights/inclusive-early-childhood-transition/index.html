Early Childhood

Transitioning From Early Intervention to Inclusive Kindergarten and School

A successful transition isn't a single enrollment day - it's a process that begins well before entry. The goal is to pass on useful knowledge about the child, experience the new environment, and continue support without the report becoming the child's identity.

By Dr. Osama Juma — PhD in Special Education, The University of Jordan · Published: August 31, 2026 · 5 min read

Key References: UNICEF: Including Children With Disabilities in Early Childhood

Why Start With a Brief Functional Profile?

Gather strengths, communication methods, what helps the child participate, difficult routines, tools used, and current goals. Focus on what the team needs to work from day one.

Get the family's consent before sharing information, and give them a role in choosing what transfers to the new school.

How Do You Make the New Environment Familiar to the Child?

Organize short visits or photos of the place, people, and daily schedule, and gradually train the child to transition, wait, and ask for help in the way that suits them.

Allow the school to observe the child in a familiar environment if possible, and coordinate between the early intervention team, the teacher, and the family instead of just handing over paperwork.

How Do You Review the Success of the First Weeks?

Identify a point of contact and a follow-up date soon after the start. Watch attendance, participation, communication, and comfort - not just academic performance alone.

Adjust support quickly if a new barrier appears, and keep successful strategies while avoiding copying the previous environment's program without testing its fit for the new classroom.

Note: This content is general awareness/educational material. Determining the most appropriate support requires understanding the student's needs and learning environment, and collaboration with the relevant team.
