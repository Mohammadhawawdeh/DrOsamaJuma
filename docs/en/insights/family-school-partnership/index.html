Family and School

The Family-School Partnership: A Practical Model for Tracking a Child's Progress

Decisions improve when the family and school exchange information regularly and focus on shared goals. An effective partnership isn't about the volume of messages - it's about clarity on what we're tracking and what we'll do based on the results.

By Dr. Osama Juma — PhD in Special Education, The University of Jordan · Published: August 31, 2026 · 5 min read

How Do You Agree on a Shared Goal?

Start with one or two goals that matter for the child and can be observed at both school and home. Write down what success means, who provides the support, and the review date.

Make sure the tone of the conversation is descriptive and respectful, and includes strengths alongside challenges.

Why Is a Brief Tracking Log Useful?

A log can include: the activity, the level of support, the outcome, and a brief note. Avoid long reports that are hard to sustain, and choose information directly tied to the goal.

The home and school environments don't need to match; what matters is understanding where the skill shows up and what factors help it.

How Does a Review Meeting End With an Actual Decision?

Start with what's improved, then review the data, and agree on a specific next step. If observations differ, treat that as information that needs interpretation, not a personal disagreement.

Close the meeting with written responsibilities and a follow-up date, so the discussion turns into implementation.

Note: This content is general awareness/educational material. Determining the most appropriate support requires understanding the student's needs and learning environment, and collaboration with the relevant team.
