Including Students With Disabilities in General School and the Community

This article by Dr. Osama Muhammad Juma was published via the INEE network on January 25, 2023. It discusses inclusion as an inherent right, traces the evolution of policy and practice, then explains the requirements that move inclusion from formal admission to genuine participation.

By Dr. Osama Juma — PhD in Special Education, The University of Jordan · Published: January 25, 2023 · 5 min read

Original source: This article was first published by the Inter-Agency Network for Education in Emergencies (INEE). Read the full original version.

Why the General School?

The article starts from the premise that the general school is the natural environment in which students with disabilities should learn alongside their peers. An inclusive school accepts diversity and gives the student an active role in learning and school life.

Inclusion isn't just being present in the building; it requires support services, age-appropriate education, and learning outcomes that account for individual needs.

How Has the Concept of Inclusion Evolved?

The article traces the global shift in focus from institutional care and segregation toward initiatives and laws supporting education in the least restrictive environment. It notes that inclusion is applied differently across countries depending on policy, school readiness, curriculum adaptation, and available support.

Despite progress in legislation and initiatives, the gap between principle and practice remains visible in many education systems, especially where accurate diagnosis and organized support for non-visible disabilities are lacking.

What Are the Foundations of Successful Inclusion?

Effective implementation focuses on the student's needs rather than their disability classification, on full access, individualized learning outcomes, participation in activities, and the teacher's role. These foundations need clear vision, planning, and shared follow-up.

The article concludes that successful inclusion needs more Arabic-language research and documentation, and requires the school, family, and community to work together to ensure a supportive environment for all students' abilities.

What Role Does the Wider Community Play?

Inclusion that stops at the classroom door is incomplete. Local sports clubs, libraries, and community centers can extend the same principles - a shared vocabulary between school and community makes it easier for a student to participate confidently in both settings.

Simple things help: a community event that shares its accessibility details in advance, or a club that asks a family what would help before assuming a student can't participate.

Note: This content is general awareness/educational material. Determining the most appropriate support requires understanding the student's needs and learning environment, and collaboration with the relevant team.
