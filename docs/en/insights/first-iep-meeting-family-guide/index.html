How to Prepare for Your First IEP Meeting: A Family Guide

A first IEP meeting can feel overwhelming with the number of attendees and the terminology. Simple preparation beforehand turns it from a situation where you receive decisions into genuine participation in making them.

By Dr. Osama Juma — PhD in Special Education, The University of Jordan · Published: September 5, 2026 · 4 min read

What Do You Do a Week Before the Meeting?

Ask for a copy of any assessments or reports the team will discuss well before the meeting - not on the same day - so you have time to read them. Write down your notes and questions in advance instead of relying on memory under pressure.

Identify one or two priorities you want the meeting to produce, such as a specific communication goal or a particular classroom accommodation, instead of trying to cover everything at once.

What Do You Do During the Meeting?

You have the right to ask for immediate clarification of any term you don't understand. Ask for every proposed goal to be linked to a concrete example: what it will look like achieved on a normal school day, and who will follow up on it.

If you feel a decision is being made without your genuine participation, it's completely acceptable to ask to postpone signing to think it over or consult someone before giving final approval.

What Do You Do After the Meeting?

Keep a signed copy of the plan, and note the next review date and how to communicate with the teacher between formal meetings. Short, regular follow-up prevents problems from piling up until the next meeting.

If you notice something in the plan isn't actually being implemented in the classroom, reach out early - there's no need to wait until the annual review date.

What Questions Should You Ask During the Meeting?

Ask how each goal will be measured, who is responsible for each support, and what happens if a strategy isn't working within a few weeks - don't wait for the next scheduled review to raise a concern.

Ask for a copy of the plan in writing before you leave, and ask directly what the school needs from the family to make the plan work. A one-sided plan rarely survives contact with a real school week.

Note: This is a general educational guide for preparing for the meeting, and does not replace reviewing the regulations and guidelines specific to your school or education authority.
