School Activities

Extracurricular Activities: Where Inclusion Is Tested Outside the Classroom

A school can be inclusive in its classrooms and still exclude students with disabilities from the trip, the sports team, or the drama club. Genuine participation needs planning that starts before the activity, not an exception made on the day.

By Dr. Osama Juma — PhD in Special Education, The University of Jordan · Published: August 31, 2026 · 5 min read

Key References: UNICEF: Inclusive Education

Why Does an Activity Exclude a Student Without Meaning To?

Registration forms, transport, changing rooms, rules, and pace are usually designed around one type of student. A hidden barrier can be more decisive than the activity's nature itself.

Ask what a specific student needs to participate before assuming they can't, and don't let staff decide on their behalf that an activity 'isn't suitable for them.'

How Do You Adapt an Activity Without Diluting It?

Adjust the rules, equipment, role, or scoring system while keeping the essence of the activity and genuine competition. A student can play a real role instead of a symbolic or spectator one.

Involve the student themselves in choosing the adaptation, since what suits one student with a similar disability may not suit another.

How Do You Build a Sustainable System, Not a One-Time Exception?

Set a clear procedure for registration, transport, and support needs that applies to every activity, and train activity supervisors, not just classroom teachers, on it.

Review participation numbers by activity type periodically, and treat a persistent gap as a signal to redesign the activity, not as evidence that certain students simply aren't interested.

Note: This content is general awareness/educational material. Determining the most appropriate support requires understanding the student's needs and learning environment, and collaboration with the relevant team.
