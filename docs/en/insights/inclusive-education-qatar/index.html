Inclusive Education in the GCC

Inclusive Education in Qatar: Practical Steps for Families

Qatar has expanded inclusion programs in public and private schools over the past decade, and many families now have genuine choices between more than one educational model.

By Dr. Osama Juma — PhD in Special Education, The University of Jordan · Published: September 6, 2026 · 4 min read

What's the Difference Between the Available Support Models?

Schools differ between those that provide full support within the general classroom, and those that rely on a resource room for part of the school day. There is no single “best” model; the right fit depends on the student's own need.

Visit the school and ask specifically how many hours the included student actually spends in the general classroom versus the support room - the ratio reveals the real nature of the inclusion more than the school's marketing description.

How Does the Family Coordinate With the School?

Ask for a regular meeting every 6 to 8 weeks instead of settling for a single meeting at the start of the year. Short, frequent follow-up catches problems early before they accumulate.

Keep a simple record of notes from each meeting and what was agreed; this makes it easier to measure progress over time.

When Is Specialized Support Outside the School Useful?

Some families combine school support with an external educational consultation to review the plan or prepare for school meetings, especially when goals feel unclear or communication has stalled.

This kind of support doesn't replace the school's role, but it helps the family ask the right questions and understand its options better.

Note: Education system details are subject to change; be sure to check with official bodies responsible for education and inclusion before making important decisions.
