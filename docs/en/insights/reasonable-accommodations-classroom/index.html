Student Support

Reasonable Classroom Accommodations: Examples and an Implementation Plan

A good accommodation addresses a specific barrier the student faces. That's why there's no single list that fits everyone - the choice starts from the task, the environment, and the communication method, then gets reviewed based on impact.

By Dr. Osama Juma — PhD in Special Education, The University of Jordan · Published: August 31, 2026 · 5 min read

Key References: Article 24 of the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities · UNICEF: Planning and Monitoring for Inclusive Education

How Do You Precisely Identify the Barrier?

Instead of saying the student can't keep up with the class, describe the situation: are the instructions too long? Is the material unreadable? Does noise prevent focus? Is the response method unsuitable?

This description helps you choose a direct fix, such as breaking instructions into parts, a readable digital format, a quieter location, or an alternative response method.

What Are Examples of Accommodations by Affected Function?

Accommodations might include extra time, structured breaks, a screen reader, enlarged text, a sign language interpreter, tactile materials, appropriate furniture, or a different schedule. The choice is individual, not based on the disability label alone.

Distinguish between adjusting how a task is accessed and changing the academic goal itself, and record that distinction clearly in the student's plan.

How Do You Implement the Accommodation, Then Review It?

Agree on who provides the accommodation, when, and where, and teach the student to use it without stigma. Collect a brief note on participation, accuracy, and independence.

If access doesn't improve, review whether the accommodation fits, whether the user was trained, and the environment. Having the tool alone doesn't guarantee it's useful.

Who Decides, and How Is It Documented?

Decisions should involve the teacher, a specialist where relevant, and the family - not one person acting alone, since each brings information the others don't have. Write the accommodation down clearly enough that a substitute teacher could follow it without further explanation.

Review documented accommodations at the start of each term, since a plan written in September for one teacher's classroom doesn't automatically transfer cleanly to a new teacher or subject.

Note: This content is general awareness/educational material. Determining the most appropriate support requires understanding the student's needs and learning environment, and collaboration with the relevant team.
