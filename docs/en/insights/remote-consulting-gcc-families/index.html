Remote Services

Remote Educational Consulting for GCC Families: How It Works and Who It's For

A family in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, or Kuwait doesn't need the educational consultant physically present in the same city to get a genuine review of their child's plan or real preparation for a school meeting.

By Dr. Osama Juma — PhD in Special Education, The University of Jordan · Published: September 6, 2026 · 4 min read

What Does a Remote Consultation Look Like in Practice?

The session usually begins with the family describing the situation over a video or voice call, sharing any existing reports or plans. The goal is to arrive at a clear next step or priority, not just a general discussion.

These sessions don't require special equipment; a phone and a regular internet connection are enough for most consultations.

Who Is This Service Specifically a Good Fit For?

This approach suits families who want to review an IEP before signing it, prepare for an important school meeting, or understand the inclusion options available before choosing a school.

This consultation doesn't replace a direct medical or psychological assessment; it complements the role of the school and local specialists rather than substituting for them.

Time Differences and Ongoing Communication

Sessions can be scheduled around the time difference between Jordan and the GCC countries, with the option of written follow-up via WhatsApp between sessions for quick questions that don't need a full session.

For schools and institutions, remote training workshops can also be organized for the teaching staff rather than limiting the service to individual consultations only.

Note: Remote educational consulting is complementary support, and does not replace a direct medical or psychological assessment or the role of official bodies in each country.
